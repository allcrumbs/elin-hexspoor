Feel-good Addictions

How games can help us save the planet

TEDx University of Ljubljana · April 2025

A note on the video

The video recording of TEDx University of Ljubljana 2025 was faulty and the video was removed. Below is the exact same speech, written down.

If I gave you two options right now, one says "go volunteering" and the other says "scroll Instagram", which would you pick? Be honest. Most people would pick Instagram. I think I would too. Not because we don't care, but because it's easier, more enjoyable, more immediate. But what if doing good felt just as easy?

I've spent the last few years studying one question: why don't we act more for society and the planet, even when we care? I studied it at university, I wrote my master's thesis on it, and today I'm building a startup with an amazing group of people to help fix it.

Here is what I've learned. Most people already care. They're just stuck. They don't know where to start, they're busy, they're tired. And TikTok and Instagram get all the attention, as opposed to, I don't know, volunteering platforms. So we scroll, we click, and three hours go by. We don't use that time to look for volunteering, to donate, to sign petitions. That is exactly what we're working on: making helping the world as addictive as scrolling, through design, psychology and play.

Part one

The real problem

Trying to activate people, I've realised it is not about willpower, or motivation for that matter. The circumstances around us shape what we do. We love to blame individuals for their habits: use less plastic, eat better, care more, be better. But in reality the environment shapes those habits long before we ever make a choice.

Imagine a cafeteria. People line up and put food on their plates. You're the manager and you want them to eat healthier. What do you do? You have options. Put the apples first and the desserts last. Swap white plates for green ones, and people subconsciously add more greens. Slow the music down, because loud fast music makes people eat more. Notice that not once did we remove any unhealthy food, and yet we influenced better choices with placement, colour and sound. No pressure, no restrictions.

In any population there are three groups: people already inclined to do the right thing, people open to a good offer, and people who will never behave the way we want. The first group finds its own way and doesn't need us. The last group only moves under law and punishment, so we set them aside. The middle group is the interesting one, and the biggest. Our goal is to move them into the first group by making contributions to society natural, intuitive and part of a daily habit.

Part two

The solutions, and the fun

What we need is to fix the circumstances around people, and the fun is the key. Lesson one: serious problems don't always need a serious solution. Fear, anger, frustration and guilt won't get you far in influencing people. So what do we do?

In a metro station in Sweden, designers turned an ordinary staircase into a working piano. Each step played a note, and almost everyone started taking the stairs instead of the escalator, just because it made them smile. It's called the fun theory. That's the power of fun: it transforms a boring task into something you want to do, not because you have to, but because it feels good.

This is gamification, using the logic of games to make life more engaging and more human. Games aren't just entertainment, they speak to our need for progress, mastery, connection and joy. So that is what we've been building: a platform where doing good feels like play. You can volunteer at an event, sign a petition, donate to a cause or plant a tree, and it's not just a list of tasks. You earn points, you build a profile, and the more good you do, the more your little digital animal grows and evolves. You see your impact, virtually and physically.

Because people are motivated by different things, we based the design on four player types: achievers who love to level up, explorers who crave discovery, socialisers who live for connection, and the competitive ones who want the leaderboard. Whatever your reason, there's a place for you.

The second secret

People move people

Another thing shapes how we behave: what everyone around us is doing. Imagine a neighbourhood, and we want them to install solar panels. Split it into three blocks, each with a different message. One block hears about protecting the planet, distant and hard to picture. The next hears about safeguarding the future for their kids. The last block just hears: "your neighbours are already doing it." No lecture, no guilt, just a gentle nudge. Behavioural science tells us that last block is the one that moves. Not because it's cheaper or greener, but because we're human, and humans follow humans. It's not peer pressure, it's peer momentum.

Think of the 2023 floods in Slovenia, when the whole country reached out, showed up in the dirt, and gave people food, clothes and shelter. That's social influence, and it's more powerful than we give it credit for. Most apps already use these same tools, likes, streaks and points, to keep us online for hours. What if we used them to help the world instead, and feel better for it?

The vision

Being addicted to doing good

Around 60% of people say they care about climate change, inequality and social injustice. They want to help, but they don't know where to start, and life gets in the way. So my vision is simple but radical: what if helping was as easy and addictive as scrolling? What if, instead of chasing likes for a new car, we chased likes for our impact?

I imagine a world where people compete to plant the most trees rather than get the most views, where volunteering becomes the new social currency, and where the tools we use every day are used not to drain us but to empower us. A world where doing good is visible, rewarding and joyful, and where you don't need to be perfect, you just need to press play.

Addiction doesn't have to be a negative thing. Imagine being addicted to contributing to your society and the world. We're closer to that than ever before.
